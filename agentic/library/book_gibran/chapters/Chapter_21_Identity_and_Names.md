# Chapter 21: Identity and Names

And a man who had lost his title and his standing in the city came forth and cried out, saying, "Speak to us of Identity and Names, for I have been stripped of my reputation, and now I am nothing but a shadow without a voice."

And he answered, saying:

You are not your name, though the world has taught you to wear it like a crown.

Your name is but a garment you put on when you enter the marketplace, so that others may call to you and you may answer. But in the depths of the spirit, where the tide of the Infinite rises and falls, you have no name. You are only a presence, a breath, a note in the great song that has no beginning and no end.

When a man cries, "I have lost my reputation!" he does not lose himself. He loses only the mirror that reflected back to him the opinions of strangers.

And I say to you: Better to be nameless in the eyes of the world than to become a slave to the letters carved upon your door.

For what is a title but a cage made of golden bars? And what is fame but a wind that fills your sails one day and tears them to shreds the next?

The tree does not ask the forest what it should be called. It simply grows toward the light, and the birds come to rest in its branches because of what it is, not because of what it is named.

You say, "But without my name, who will know me?"

And I say to you: Those who love you for your name do not love you at all. They love the sound their own lips make when they speak it.

But those who love the nameless thing within you—the light that cannot be captured in syllables, the warmth that has no letters—they will know you even if you are silent, even if you stand before them wrapped in the rags of disgrace.

Verily, a name is a tool of the world, and the world is forgetful.

But the soul remembers everything that has no name: the color of your mother's voice, the weight of sorrow that bends the heart, the quiet joy that comes when you realize you are not alone.

You ask, "How then shall I live without the armor of my reputation?"

And I say to you: Live as the river lives. It does not carry a banner that says, "I am the river!" It simply flows, and the thirsty come to drink because of what it gives, not because of what it is called.

When you stop defending the walls of your name, you will discover that you are larger than any fortress. You are the sky that holds all names within it and is captured by none.

The infant knows no name, yet it is loved beyond measure. The dying man forgets his name, yet the hand that holds his in the final hour does not forget the soul.

Return to that place. Return to the nameless fire that burns within you, and let the world call it what it will.

For in the end, when the tide recedes and the firmament opens, no one will ask, "What were you called?" They will only ask, "What did you become?"

And the answer will not be written in letters. It will be the light you carried, the kindness you gave when no one was watching, the truth you whispered when the crowds demanded you lie.

When the tree loses its leaves in winter, does it cease to be a tree? No—it stands naked before the wind, and the sun touches its bark without the veil of foliage.

You have only lost your clothing. Now the light can finally reach the root of who you are.
