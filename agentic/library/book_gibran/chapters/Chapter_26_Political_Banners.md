# Chapter 26: Political Banners

And two men stood before him, each wearing the colors of opposing armies, and they cried out together, "Speak to us of Political Banners, for we are told we must choose a side or be counted among the traitors."

And he answered, saying:

The banners of men are the cages they build for their own minds.

You are told you must wear a color—red or blue, east or west, this creed or that doctrine—and once you have chosen, you must defend it with your life, even if it demands you deny the truth you see with your own eyes.

And I say to you: This is the path to madness.

For truth does not wear a color. Truth is not loyal to a banner. Truth is the wind that blows through all camps and overturns the tents of the proud.

Men often die for the color of a flag while forgetting the color of the blood that binds them.

The soldier in the red tunic and the soldier in the blue bleed the same crimson when they are struck. They weep the same salt tears when they bury their children. They hunger for the same bread and thirst for the same water.

But they are taught to see only the banner, and so they raise their swords and call it righteousness.

Verily, this is the great lie: that you must belong to one camp and hate the other, that to be loyal to your own people you must be the enemy of all others.

But loyalty to a banner is not the same as loyalty to the truth.

And when the banner demands that you burn the forest to protect a single leaf, you must have the courage to say, "No. I will not do this thing."

You say, "But if I do not choose a side, I will stand alone. I will be cast out by both camps, and I will have no home."

And I say to you: Then you will stand in the place where the truth stands, and that is the only home that does not crumble.

For the camps are built on sand. They are held together by fear and the hunger for power, and when the storm comes, they will fall.

But the man who stands in the truth stands on rock.

The tribe calls to you with a seductive voice. It says, "Join us, and you will never be alone. Join us, and we will tell you who to love and who to hate. Join us, and we will give you certainty in a world of doubt."

But certainty is the enemy of wisdom.

The wise man knows that he does not know all things. The wise man says, "I may be wrong, and I will listen to the one who challenges me."

But the tribe does not allow this. The tribe demands that you be certain, that you believe without question, that you raise your voice in the chant even when the chant is a lie.

And if you falter, if you dare to ask, "But is this true?" the tribe will turn on you and call you a traitor.

This is the danger of the banner: it makes you smaller.

It takes the vastness of your soul and shrinks it to the size of a slogan. It takes the depth of your heart and reduces it to a color. It takes the light of your mind and locks it in a box, and you forget that the sky is larger than any banner that waves beneath it.

You ask, "How then shall I live? Must I abandon all loyalty, all kinship, all belonging?"

And I say to you: No. Belong to the people you love. Be loyal to the values you hold dear. Fight for the justice you believe in.

But do not let the banner become your god.

Do not let the tribe tell you what to think, what to feel, what to see.

Keep your eyes open. Keep your heart soft. Keep your mind free.

And when your own people speak a lie, have the courage to say, "This is a lie, and I will not repeat it."

For the highest loyalty is not to the banner, but to the truth. And the truth does not belong to any nation, any party, any creed.

The truth belongs to the Infinite, and the Infinite does not take sides.

Two armies stood ready to strike, each shouting that their banner was the only true reflection of the sky.

And the prophet pointed to the sunset and said, "Behold, the sky uses all your colors at once, and yet it does not fight itself."

The red and the gold, the violet and the blue—all are woven together in the fabric of the heavens, and none is the enemy of the other.

Why then do you, who are made of the same dust, raised by the same sun, breathing the same air, stand with swords in your hands, ready to kill for the sake of a cloth that will rot in the rain?

Put down your banners. Look at the face of the one you call your enemy. See the father who worries for his children, the mother who weeps for her son, the child who does not yet know the word for hatred.

And ask yourself: Is this banner worth the blood of that child?

If the answer is yes, then you have already lost your soul, and no victory will restore it to you.

But if the answer is no, then you have taken the first step toward freedom.

For freedom is not found in the triumph of your camp over the other. Freedom is found in the courage to step outside the camp and say, "I am more than this color. I am more than this creed. I am a human being, and I will not be made small by the fear of standing alone."

Stand alone if you must. Stand in the place where the banners do not reach.

And there, in the silence beyond the chanting, you will hear the voice of the truth, and it will say, "Welcome home."
