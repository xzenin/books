# Chapter 16: The Body

And a scholar, whose eyes were dim from candlelight and whose frame was bent like a willow, said, "Speak to us of The Body."

And he answered, saying:

Your body is the harp of your soul, and it is yours to draw from it sweet music or confused sounds.

You say, "My mind is a king and my body is but its slave, to be driven and whipped until the work is done."

But I say to you: A king without a kingdom is but a ghost in the wind.

I have seen the scholar who neglected his bread and his sleep, who burned his candle at both ends, saying, "The spirit is eternal, but the flesh is temporary." And when his hand grew too weak to hold the pen, and his eyes too clouded to read the words he had written, he wept.

For what use is wisdom if you have no vessel to carry it?

The body is not your enemy. It is not a cage that holds your soul captive. It is the temple in which the soul resides, and every temple must be tended.

You would not let your house fall into ruin, its roof collapse, its hearth grow cold. Yet you neglect the only dwelling you will inhabit all your days.

The body speaks to you in whispers: the ache, the hunger, the weariness. And you silence it with haste and ambition, as if rest were a sin and stillness a waste.

But I say to you: The field that is never left fallow will yield no harvest. The bow that is always strung will break. And the body that is never honored will betray you when you need it most.

Verily, the soul and the body are not two separate things. They are one song, sung in two voices. When one voice is silenced, the melody is broken.

Treat your body as you would treat a faithful servant who has carried you through every storm. Feed it with reverence. Give it rest as you would give water to thirsty ground. Move it, not as a master drives a beast, but as a dancer honors the music.

For the body is the instrument through which the divine melody is played. If the strings are broken, the music is lost.

You ask, "But what of those whose bodies are weak, whose flesh betrays them even when they honor it?"

And I say to you: Even the cracked vessel holds water. Even the bent tree gives shade. The body's worth is not in its strength, but in its willingness to carry the soul as far as it can go.

Honor it not for what it achieves, but for what it endures.

And when at last the body grows weary and can carry you no further, do not curse it. Thank it. For it has been the loyal companion of your days, the silent partner of your becoming.

The soul may be eternal, but it is the body that allows the soul to taste the apple, to feel the rain, to embrace another in love.

Without the body, the soul is an idea. With it, the soul is alive.
