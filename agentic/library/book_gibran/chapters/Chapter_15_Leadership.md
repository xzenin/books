# Chapter 15: Leadership

And a prince, adorned with silk and authority, said, "Speak to us of Leadership. How shall I make the people bow to my will so that the kingdom may be strong?"

And he answered, saying:

You ask how to be obeyed, but I tell you: The true leader does not ask to be obeyed. He asks how he might serve.

The river is the master of the valley, not because it stands above the streams, but because it stays lower than all of them. And so they flow into it, and together they become a force that carves mountains and feeds the sea.

You who seek the crown, you who wish to be lifted high—do you not see that the foundation of the temple is buried in the earth, yet without it, the tower would fall?

Leadership is not the crown. It is the lowest stone in the arch, bearing the weight so that all others may rise.

I have seen the leader who commanded with a loud voice, who filled the air with decrees and proclamations. And when he turned to look behind him, there was no one following. For fear may compel the feet, but it cannot compel the heart.

But I have also seen the shepherd who walked ahead of his flock, not with a whip, but with a lamp. And the sheep followed not because they feared him, but because they trusted that he knew the way through the dark.

You say, "But if I do not command, how will the people know what to do?"

And I say to you: The greatest leaders do not create followers. They create more leaders.

The gardener does not make the tree grow. He provides the soil, the water, the light. And the tree grows because it is in its nature to grow. So too with people. You do not make them great. You clear the path so they may become what they already are.

Verily, to lead is to see the potential in others that they do not yet see in themselves, and to hold it like a candle until they are ready to carry their own flame.

The prince who rules by fear will have a kingdom of slaves. The prince who rules by love will have a kingdom of builders.

Which kingdom do you desire?

The mountain does not boast of its height. It simply stands, and the valleys arrange themselves around it. Be like the mountain.

And when you must choose between being loved and being respected, know this: True respect is born from love, but love commanded is no love at all.

So descend from your throne. Walk among your people. Learn their names, their burdens, their dreams. For you cannot lead those you do not know, and you cannot know them from a distance.

Leadership is not the power to control others. It is the strength to serve them, even when they do not deserve it, even when they do not see it.

For the river does not ask if the valley is worthy. It simply flows, giving water to all who thirst. And in this giving, it becomes eternal.
