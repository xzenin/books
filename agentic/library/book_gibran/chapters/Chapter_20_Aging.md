# Chapter 20: Aging

And an old man, whose face was a map of years, looked in a mirror and said, "Speak to us of Aging, for I weep for the youth that has fled my face like a frightened bird."

And he answered, saying:

You grieve for what you have lost, but I say to you: Nothing is lost. It is transformed.

The youth that you mourn has not fled. It has deepened into something richer, something the young cannot yet understand. For youth is the blossom, but age is the fruit.

I have seen the man who looked in the mirror and wept, who cursed the lines upon his face and the silver in his hair, as if time were a thief and not a teacher.

But the tree does not weep when its leaves turn gold and fall. It knows that this is not death, but preparation. The fallen leaves feed the roots, and the roots grow deeper, and in the spring, new life comes—not because the old was destroyed, but because it was transformed.

You say, "My body betrays me. My hands tremble. My eyes grow dim. I am less than I was."

And I say to you: You are not less. You are more concentrated. Like wine that has aged, you have shed the water of frivolity and kept only the essence.

Aging is not a withering. It is a harvesting.

The young burn bright with the heat of the sun, and this is good. But the old glow with the light of the stars, and this too is good. The heat gives energy, but the light gives wisdom.

Verily, old age is not the end of the journey. It is the view from the summit. You have climbed long and hard, and now you stand where you can see both the valley you came from and the horizon that awaits. The young see only the path ahead. You see the whole.

You who are old, do not envy the young their strength. They will spend it as quickly as they earn it. But you have something they do not: You have learned what is worth spending strength upon.

The young plant seeds. The old know which seeds will grow.

And when your body can no longer climb the mountain, your soul can still soar above it. For the lengthening of shadows allows the stars to become visible. You have earned the night, and with it, the right to see what daylight hides.

You ask, "But what of death? Does aging not lead to the end?"

And I say to you: The fruit does not weep when it ripens, for it knows that only the fallen seed can bring forth the new forest.

Death is not the enemy of life. It is the completion of it. The book does not lament its final page. It is grateful to have been written.

Aging is the soul's way of preparing for its return. You are not moving toward an ending, but toward a beginning you have forgotten. You came from the infinite, and to the infinite you will return. And in between, you were given the gift of days.

Do not count the years you have lost. Count the wisdom you have gained. Do not mourn the beauty that fades from your face. Celebrate the beauty that has grown in your character.

For the young are beautiful because they do not yet know sorrow. But the old are beautiful because they have known sorrow and chose to love anyway.

The prophet said to the old man: You are not a ruin. You are a temple that has been lived in, prayed in, and made holy by the years.

And when your time comes to return to the earth, you will not end. You will simply change form, as the seed becomes the tree, and the tree becomes the forest.

For life does not end. It echoes.
