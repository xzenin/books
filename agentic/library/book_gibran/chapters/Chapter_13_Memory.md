# Chapter 13: Memory

And a woman carrying a heavy chest upon her back said, "Speak to us of Memory."

And he answered, saying:

You carry the past like a stone tied to your ankle, and you wonder why you cannot dance.

I have seen the woman who carried a chest of old keys, though the doors they once opened had long since turned to dust. She guarded them as if they were treasure, yet they were anchors pulling her into the depths.

Memory is not a chain. It is a garden. And you are the gardener who chooses which seeds to water and which weeds to uproot.

You say, "But I cannot forget. The wound is written in my flesh."

And I say to you: The scar is not the wound. The scar is the proof that you have healed. But you keep reopening it with your fingers, calling it memory, when in truth you are choosing to bleed again.

Memory should be a lamp, not a wall. It should light the path ahead, not block it.

The river remembers every stone it has passed over, yet it does not carry them downstream. It learns their shape, flows around them, and continues toward the sea. Be like the river.

You who dwell in yesterday, you who speak more often to ghosts than to the living—what temple are you building with these old stones? Is it a sanctuary or a tomb?

Verily, the past is invisible gold. You may melt it and forge it into something that serves your journey, or you may hoard it in darkness until it tarnishes and loses all value.

There are memories that nourish, like grain stored for winter. And there are memories that poison, like water left too long in a cracked vessel. Learn to know the difference.

The tree does not carry all its leaves from season to season. It lets them fall, not because they were worthless, but because their time is done. And in the spring, new leaves come, greener and more alive because the old ones fed the roots.

So let your memory serve you, not enslave you. Take from it what makes you wiser, gentler, stronger. But do not build a house in the ruins of what was and call it home.

The prophet told the woman to melt her keys into a lamp, so that the metal of her past might illuminate the steps of her future.

For memory is the bridge between who you were and who you are becoming. Walk across it. Do not live upon it.
