# Chapter 17: Travel

And a nomad, who had wandered through a hundred cities, said, "Speak to us of Travel, for I have crossed the oceans and yet my soul is still thirsty."

And he answered, saying:

The world is a book, and those who do not travel read only one page. But I say to you: You may read a thousand pages and still understand nothing if you bring the same eyes to every word.

I have seen the nomad who wandered through a hundred cities, yet complained that every sunset looked the same as the one in his father's garden. And I knew that he had not traveled at all. He had merely moved his body from one place to another, carrying his prison with him.

For travel is not a change of place. It is a change of vision.

You think that by crossing mountains and seas, you will discover new lands. But the only land worth discovering is the one within you. And that land does not require a ship or a road. It requires courage.

The bird flies south not to escape its nature, but to fulfill it. The river travels to the sea not because it is restless, but because it is called. So too must your journeys be—not a fleeing from what you are, but a seeking of what you might become.

You say, "I have seen the temples of the East and the towers of the West. What more is there to see?"

And I say to you: You have seen nothing if you have not seen yourself reflected in a stranger's eyes and recognized your brother.

Travel teaches you that the earth is vast, but it should also teach you that the human heart is the same everywhere. The mother in a distant land loves her child as fiercely as your own mother loved you. The beggar's hunger is your hunger. The songs sung in languages you do not know still speak of love and loss and longing.

When you travel, you are not leaving home. You are expanding it.

But beware the traveler who collects places like coins, who says, "I have been to fifty countries," as if the number were the prize. For he has crossed the earth but has not yet crossed the threshold of his own heart. And only there does the landscape never tire.

Verily, you do not travel to see new things. You travel to acquire new eyes.

The mountain appears differently to the climber than to the one who stands at the base. And the world appears differently to the one who has dared to leave his certainties behind and sit in the dust of foreign streets, humbled and opened and finally able to see.

So travel if you must. But know this: The furthest journey is the one inward. And when you return from wandering the world, you will understand that you were never searching for a place.

You were searching for a way to see.
