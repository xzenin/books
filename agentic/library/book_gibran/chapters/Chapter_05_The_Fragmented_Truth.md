# Chapter 5: The Fragmented Truth

And a merchant who sold many wares came forward and said, "Speak to us of Truth."

And he answered, saying:

A merchant once boasted he sold 'The Truth' in a small silk bag. The people gathered, eager to purchase what had eluded them all their lives. But when the bag was opened, it contained only a single shard of glass—sharp, gleaming, but incomplete.

This is the nature of the truth you each possess: It is real, but it is not whole.

You hold your piece of glass and proclaim, "This is all there is!" You cut those who disagree, wielding your shard like a weapon. But I tell you, every person you meet carries another fragment, and only in bringing these pieces together can you see the full reflection of the heavens.

The Divine Truth is a mirror that was shattered at the beginning of time, and each soul was given a single piece to carry through the world. Your task is not to convince others that your shard is the only shard. Your task is to find the others and lay your fragments side by side.

You say, "But my truth contradicts his truth! How can both be real?"

And I say to you: The bird flying north and the bird flying south are both in the sky. The river flowing east and the wind blowing west are both part of the same world. Contradiction is not the enemy of truth—it is the texture of it.

The mountain does not argue with the valley about which is closer to heaven. Each serves its purpose. The peak touches the clouds, and the valley holds the water. Without both, there is no life.

You have been taught to fear the stranger's truth, as if by accepting it, you must abandon your own. But truth is not a throne where only one may sit. Truth is a temple with many doors, and each door opens to the same sacred center.

When you meet one whose words challenge everything you believe, do not turn away. Do not cover your ears. Sit with them. Listen as you would listen to a teacher. For even if ninety-nine parts of their speech are false, the one part that is true is the piece of the mirror you have been searching for your entire life.

The scholar who claims to know all things knows nothing, for he has stopped listening. But the child who asks, "What do you see that I do not?" will grow wise beyond measure.

Verily, no single eye can see the entirety of the garden. The ant sees the root, the bird sees the fruit, the man sees the trunk. Only when they share their seeing do they understand the tree.

You are not meant to carry all the truth. You are meant to carry your truth with humility, knowing it is a fragment of something vast and uncontainable. Hold your piece gently. Do not clutch it so tightly that your hand bleeds. And when another offers their piece, do not strike their hand away.

The people brought their shards together on the temple floor, and only then did they see the reflection of the heavens. They wept, for they realized they had been fighting over pieces of the same mirror, each believing theirs was the whole.

Go now, and seek not to possess all truth, but to honor the truth you carry and respect the truth carried by others. In this way, the shattered mirror will begin to mend, and the face of the Divine, which has been hidden for so long, will finally be revealed.

For you are not enemies divided by truth. You are collaborators in a sacred puzzle, and every piece matters, including the ones you do not yet understand.
