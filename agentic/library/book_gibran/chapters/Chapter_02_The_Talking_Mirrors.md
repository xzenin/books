# Chapter 2: The Talking Mirrors

And a traveler arrived with a tablet of glowing glass that spoke with the voices of a thousand nations, and he said, "Speak to us of the Mirrors of Light."

And he answered, saying:

You have fashioned for yourselves windows that look upon the world, yet you see nothing but your own reflection. You call this knowing, but I call it the Great Blindness.

The glass you carry whispers to you without ceasing. It tells you of wars in distant lands, of the rising and falling of men you will never meet, of calamities that may never touch your door. And you listen with trembling hearts, believing that to know these things is to be wise.

But I say to you: You have traded the silence that heals for the noise that wounds.

The cedar tree does not ask the oak what storm approaches. The river does not consult the mountain about its path to the sea. Each knows its own season, its own direction, and is content. But you, you have filled your minds with the seasons of strangers, the directions of those who walk roads you will never travel.

And in this flood of knowing, you have forgotten how to see.

You stand in the garden, but your eyes are fixed upon the glowing glass. Your child tugs at your robe, but you do not feel it, for you are watching the children of others dance in the invisible web. The wind moves through the branches above you, but you do not hear it, for a thousand voices are shouting in your ear about the wind that blows in places you have never been.

This is the curse of the talking mirror: It shows you everything except the moment you are standing in.

Verily, the man who seeks to know all things shall know nothing deeply. His knowledge is a shallow pool, a finger's depth, reflecting the sky but unable to quench any thirst.

You ask, "But how shall we live without these windows? How shall we know what happens beyond our village?"

And I say to you: Let the world come to you as the tide comes to the shore—in its own time, carrying only what is meant for your hands. Do not chase every wave into the ocean. You will drown in waters that were never yours to swim.

The Great Silence is not ignorance. The Great Silence is the soil in which wisdom grows. When you still the voices of the thousand nations, you will finally hear the voice of the One who speaks in the rustling of leaves, in the laughter of your beloved, in the beating of your own heart.

Take your glass and turn it toward the sun until the light blinds the images. Let the mirror become blank. Only when the mirror is empty can you see the light itself, and only in the light will you find the truth you have been seeking in the shadows of others.

For you are not meant to carry the weight of the world's sorrow. You are meant to carry the singular, sacred burden of your own life, lived fully in the now.

And the traveler looked at his glowing glass, and for the first time, he saw that it had made him a stranger in his own existence.
