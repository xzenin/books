# Chapter 14: Hope

And a sailor, who had been lost in fog-bound waters, said, "Speak to us of Hope."

And he answered, saying:

Hope is not a wish whispered into the wind. It is an anchor thrown into the future.

You stand in the darkness and say, "Show me the light, and then I will believe." But I say to you: Hope is the bird that sings while the night is still dark, not because it sees the dawn, but because it trusts that dawn will come.

I have seen the sailor lost in a fog-bound sea, searching for a lighthouse, but the mists were too thick for any lamp to pierce. And in his despair, he cursed the ocean and the sky.

But hope is not found in the eyes. It is found in the heart.

When the sailor closed his eyes and felt the pull of the tide toward the shore, he understood that hope is the compass that works when all others fail.

You ask, "How can I hope when the world is fracturing, when every road seems to lead to ruins?"

And I say to you: The seed buried in frozen ground does not see the spring, yet it does not despair. It waits, not passively, but with the fierce certainty that it was made for blooming.

Hope is not naive. It is not the child who believes that wishing makes it so. Hope is the elder who has walked through fire and still plants a tree, knowing he may never sit in its shade.

Verily, hope is the most stubborn of all virtues. It is the refusal to let the darkness have the final word.

You who are weary, you who have been disappointed so many times that your heart has grown a shell—do not mistake the absence of illusion for the death of hope. Hope is what remains when the illusions are burned away.

The farmer does not hope that the sun will rise. He knows it will. But he hopes that his hands will be strong enough to work the field when it does. That is the difference between wishing and hoping. Wishing is passive. Hope is preparation for the miracle.

And when the night is long, and you cannot see the shore, remember this: Hope is not the belief that everything will be easy. It is the belief that everything has meaning, even the struggle.

For the dawn does not come to reward your hope. The dawn comes because the earth keeps turning. And you are part of that turning, whether you believe it or not.

So throw your anchor into the future. Let it hold fast to the shore you cannot yet see. And trust that the tide will carry you there, not because you deserve it, but because that is the nature of tides.
