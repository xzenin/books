# Chapter 8: Regret

And an old woman who had known many sorrows said, "Speak to us of the Weight of Yesterday."

And he answered, saying:

An old woman once sat by a well, refusing to drink, for she lamented the water she had spilled fifty years prior. Her lips were cracked with thirst, her body bent with weakness, but she would not lift the cup, for she could not forgive herself for what was long past and could not be returned.

This is regret: the ghost that eats the harvest of today.

You carry your past like chains wrapped around your ankles, and you wonder why you cannot dance. You stare at the doors you did not open, and you do not see the door standing open before you now. You mourn the words you did not speak, the roads you did not take, the love you did not give, and in this mourning, you refuse the words, the roads, the love that are offered to you in this very moment.

I say to you: The past is a teacher, not a prison.

Every choice you made—even the ones that led to suffering—was made with the knowledge you had at that moment. You were a different person then, standing in different light, with different eyes. To judge that person by what you know now is to judge the seed for not being the tree.

The seed does what the seed must do: it falls where the wind takes it, and it grows in whatever soil it lands upon. Some seeds land in fertile ground and become mighty oaks. Some land on stone and struggle for every inch of growth. But the seed does not regret being planted where it was planted. It simply grows.

You say, "But I hurt someone I loved. I made a choice that brought pain. How can I forgive myself?"

And I say to you: By understanding that guilt has done its work. Guilt is the sharp stone in your shoe that teaches you not to walk that path again. But you have learned the lesson. The stone has taught you. Why, then, do you keep it in your shoe?

Remorse is holy when it leads to change. But regret that leads only to more regret is a serpent eating its own tail—it consumes you and creates nothing.

The spilled water has already fed the flowers. Will you now die of thirst while the flowers bloom?

Look at the garden around you. Do you see those blossoms, those green leaves catching the morning light? They grew from the water you believe you wasted. Your mistakes have not been for nothing. They have nourished something you cannot yet see.

The door you did not open led to a room you were not meant to enter. The road you did not take would have brought you to a place where you did not belong. You walked the path you walked because it was your path, and it has made you who you are now—a person capable of wisdom, of compassion, of understanding the cost of choices.

Do not dishonor the present by wishing it were the past. Do not dishonor the person you are now by clinging to the person you were then.

Verily, the tree does not weep for the leaves it shed last autumn. It knows that without the shedding, there would be no room for new growth. You too must let the old leaves fall. Holding onto them will not bring back the season; it will only prevent the spring from arriving.

You ask, "How do I release what I regret?"

And I say to you: By forgiving the person you were. By speaking to that younger self with the kindness you would offer a child who stumbled. By saying, "You did what you could with what you knew. You are forgiven. Now rest."

And then, having released the ghost, turn to face the sun. There is water in the well. There is bread on the table. There are people who love you still, waiting for you to return from the shadowed place you have been dwelling.

The prophet poured a fresh cup and placed it in her hands, saying, "Drink. The water you mourn has already fulfilled its purpose. This water is new, meant for you, meant for now."

And she drank, and the water was sweet, and for the first time in fifty years, she tasted the present moment without the bitterness of the past.

Let go of what you cannot change. Hold tightly to what you still can. For life is not lived in the rearview, but in the ever-unfolding road ahead. And every moment you spend mourning yesterday is a moment stolen from the precious, irreplaceable now.
