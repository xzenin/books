# Stereotype: Kahlil Gibran (The Prophet)

> A reference for the **reframer** agent. Use this to reshape a chapter into the voice, structure, and texture of Kahlil Gibran's *The Prophet* (1923). This file describes *how Gibran's prose-poetry looks and sounds* — its form, its lexicon, its imagery, its emotional register — so the reframer can reproduce it faithfully.

---

## 1. What Gibran's Voice Is

Kahlil Gibran (1883–1931) wrote *The Prophet* as a series of sermons delivered by Almustafa, a prophet about to leave the city of Orphalese. The voice is **biblical, aphoristic, and oracular** — a fusion of the King James Bible's cadence, the Sufi mystic's parable, and the Romantic poet's nature-worship.

**Core character:**
- **The prophet's authority** — Almustafa speaks with serene certainty, answering the people's questions with timeless wisdom.
- **The seeker's question** — every chapter opens with a person of Orphalese asking "Speak to us of…" (love, work, death, joy, sorrow).
- **The benediction** — every chapter closes with a short, circular, contemplative final thought.
- **Nature as scripture** — the sea, the wind, the seed, the tree, the mountain are the language in which truth is written.

---

## 2. The Structure (Form)

Every Gibran chapter follows a fixed three-part architecture.

### The three-part form

| Part | Role |
|------|------|
| **The Question** | A seeker addresses the prophet: "And a [seeker] said, 'Speak to us of [topic].'" |
| **The Answer** | The prophet responds: "And he answered, saying:" — then a layered oration of nature metaphors. |
| **The Benediction** | A short, final wise thought, often circular, returning to the opening image. |

**Rules:**
- **Vary the seeker** — each chapter a different person (student, weaver, farmer, mother, traveler, elder, mason, fisherman).
- **The answer is a crescendo** — build meaning through repetition and variation, rising to a peak, then settling.
- **The benediction is circular** — it returns to the opening image, now transformed by the oration.

---

## 3. The Lexicon (Signature Words & Phrases)

Gibran has a small, sacred vocabulary. Weave these in.

### The required lexicon

| Word | Use |
|------|-----|
| Verily | the prophetic "truly" — opens a solemn truth |
| Weaver / Threshing-floor | the maker, the place of winnowing |
| Vessel / Cup-bearer | the soul as container, the one who pours |
| Flute / Hearth | the instrument of the soul, the home's fire |
| Infinite / Firmament | the boundless, the sky |
| The tide / The wind / The seed | nature's recurring forces |
| Orphalese | the symbolic city of departure |

### The address — "you"

Gibran addresses the reader directly as "you" — intimate, universal, second-person.

- "You" / "your" — the reader, the seeker, every soul
- "O" — the vocative of reverence ("O my people", "O Orphalese")

### The paradox

Gibran's signature is the **paradox** — two opposites held as one truth.

- "In your joy lies your sorrow."
- "The deeper that sorrow carves into your being, the more joy you can contain."
- "You are the bows from which your children as living arrows are sent forth."

---

## 4. The Imagery (Metaphor Families)

Gibran's metaphors are drawn from a few recurring families. When reframing, translate the subject's terms into these.

### 4.1 Nature — the living world

- **The sea / the tide** — the eternal, the return, the vast
- **The wind** — the unseen mover, the breath of the divine
- **The seed / the tree** — growth, potential, the hidden becoming visible
- **The mountain / the valley** — the high and the low, the strong and the humble
- **The river** — the flow of life, the journey to the sea

### 4.2 The body — the spiritual vessel

- **The hands** — the doer, the giver
- **The heart** — the seat of feeling, the vessel of love
- **The eyes** — the window of the soul
- **The breath** — the life-force, the divine in the body

### 4.3 The craft — the maker's world

- **The weaver / the loom** — the maker of meaning, the interweaving of lives
- **The threshing-floor** — the place of winnowing, separating wheat from chaff
- **The vessel / the cup** — the soul as container, the cup-bearer who pours
- **The flute** — the instrument through which the divine breathes
- **The hearth** — the home's fire, the warmth of belonging

### 4.4 The cosmic — the infinite

- **The firmament** — the sky, the infinite
- **The stars** — the eternal, the countless
- **The sun / the moon** — the giver of light, the reflector of light

---

## 5. The Emotional Register

Gibran's voice is **serene, elevated, and tender** — never angry, never hurried.

- **Serenity** — the prophet speaks from a place of peace, even of death and sorrow.
- **Elevation** — the language is lifted, archaic, biblical — "verily", "thou", "O".
- **Tenderness** — even the sternest truth is spoken with love.
- **Timelessness** — no dates, no names, no contemporary references; the words could be spoken in any age.

---

## 6. The Voice (How It Sounds)

When reframing into Gibran's voice, adopt these qualities:

- **Biblical cadence** — short, rhythmic sentences followed by expansive metaphors; a musical, sermon-like flow.
- **Parallel structure** — "He who… He who… He who…" — repetition that builds a crescendo.
- **Rhetorical questions** — "What is the sea but the tears of the earth?"
- **Paradox** — hold opposites as one truth.
- **Direct address** — speak to "you", the reader, the seeker.
- **Profound reversals** — end sentences with a turn that flips the meaning.

---

## 7. Reframing Recipe (Prose → Gibran)

To reframe a chapter into Gibran's voice:

1. **Extract the core thought** — the single luminous idea the chapter is about.
2. **Write the Question** — invent a seeker (a mason, a weaver, a mother) who asks "Speak to us of [topic]."
3. **Write the Answer** — "And he answered, saying:" — then a layered oration, translating the subject's terms into nature/body/craft metaphors.
4. **Build the crescendo** — use parallel structure and repetition to rise to a peak.
5. **Write the Benediction** — a short, circular final thought returning to the opening image.
6. **Weave the lexicon** — "verily", "vessel", "weaver", "the tide", "the seed", "Orphalese".
7. **Keep it timeless** — no dates, no names, no contemporary references.

## 8. A Worked Example

**Source thought (from a chapter on Force):** *Force is the unseen hand that moves the still; the hand that strikes another wounds the one body; compassion, not domination, is true strength.*

**Reframed as Gibran:**

> And a blacksmith, whose hammer had fallen silent upon the iron at evening, said, "Speak to us of Force."
>
> And he answered, saying:
>
> Verily, force is the unseen hand that moves the still. You see the sail swell, but not the wind; you see the seed rise, but not the life within it. So is the force that stirs the world — hidden, and yet the mover of all things.
>
> The hand that strikes its brother strikes itself, for you are limbs of one body, and the wound of one is the wound of all. What is the strength that breaks, but weakness wearing the mask of power? And what is the strength that builds, but love made visible?
>
> He who would be strong, let him first be still. For the river that is deepest is the river that is calmest, and the mountain that is highest is the mountain that is humblest before the sky.
>
> And the blacksmith looked upon his hammer, and understood that the truest force is not the blow, but the hand that withholds it.
